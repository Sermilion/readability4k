GameStop lists used PS5 Pro for $500 more than a new console

PopularGameStop lists a used PS5 Pro for $1,399.99, $500 above its listed new price, while an apparent internal memo describes the pricing as intentional.

VIEW GALLERY - 3
Jak Connor
Tech and Science Editor
Published Oct 6, 2026 3:03 AM CDT
1 minute & 15 seconds read time

What you need to know

GameStop lists a preowned PS5 Pro at $1,399.99, $500 above its listed new price of $899.99, though the used console was marked unavailable online. A circulating, unverified memo says the higher pricing is intentional, but its authenticity and timing are uncertain.

Voice: Jak Connor

GameStop's US website lists a preowned PS5 Pro for $1,399.99, putting the used console $500 above the retailer's listed price for a new unit.

The same product page displays $899.99 under the new condition option, while advertising a preowned price of $1,329.99 for GameStop Pro members. Even with that discount, the used system carries a $430 premium over the listed new price. The preowned item was marked unavailable online when checked, so the listing does not establish that shoppers can currently purchase it at the advertised price.

The unusual pricing matches part of an apparent internal GameStop memo shared by Nintendeal on X, which reveals it is intentional. The document lists preowned digital PS5 Pro configurations at $1,399.99 and configurations labeled "Physical" at $1,499.99. Its table also includes 30th Anniversary and Wolverine editions, with prices following the same digital and physical split. According to the document, the corresponding trade values would be $800 and $850.

3
VIEW GALLERY - 3 IMAGES

The memo explicitly states that "the preowned console will have a higher price than the new console," adding that "this is intentional." However, that explanation remains attributed to the circulating document rather than an independently authenticated company statement, meaning there is a chance it could be fake.

There is also a timing discrepancy which lends to the theory the memo is fake, as the document describes October 16 as "tomorrow," despite circulating ahead of that date. GameStop's product page corroborates the $1,399.99 asking price, but does not confirm the memo's implementation schedule or every configuration listed.
